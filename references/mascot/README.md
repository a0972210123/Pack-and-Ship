### Locked direction

- The character's name is **Telo（特羅）**.
- A cute cartoon creature grown from the Tela Aurea Lab logo, not a literal
  splice of two animals.
- Fusion balance: structurally more pangolin, with armour and the ability to
  curl; emotionally and in silhouette, more lion through an outward mane and a
  regal presence.
- The armour is resilient rather than rigid. Mane plates can lag, sway and
  flick slightly, giving a lion-mane read without becoming fur.
- Personality: lively and agile, curious, innocent and eager to share.
- It reads as an early teenager, roughly **12–15**, with youthful drive,
  impulsive forward energy and no toddler/chibi coding.
- It normally stands upright with a kaiju-like body shape and stance, using
  Godzilla or Groudon only as morphology shorthand rather than as visual-design
  references. Its actual movement is agile, quick and energetic—not a slow,
  heavy giant-monster walk.
- Its lazy locomotion is a signature action: a small preparatory hop, a fast
  curl into a ball, then rolling to the destination.
- The face is pangolin-first. It has a slightly pointed, subtly upturned snout.
  From the front it should read as feline; in profile it should read as a
  pangolin. Domestic-cat scale and contours are the temporary baseline for the
  nose, brow ridge and cheeks.
- Eyes are medium-large rounded almonds. Their pupils normally stay enlarged,
  giving a feline, curious presence. When the pupils constrict they remain round,
  never changing into vertical cat slits.
- Ears are small rounded triangles. At rest they angle slightly upward and
  outward in the owner's intended mild "airplane ear" pose; surprise and
  excitement lift them fully upright.
- The nose is small, matte and dark gray. The mouth uses a minimal line.
- The neutral expression is gently happy: brow bones slightly raised and the
  mouth held in a subtle smile. The range and exaggeration of later facial
  deformation remain open.
- Baseline height is **4.75 heads**, with minor adjustment allowed after
  silhouette testing. Re-evaluate it against the 12–15-year-old age read, but
  keep the character adolescent rather than toddler-like.
- The legs are short, thick and powerful. Their shape carries the monster-like
  morphology, while posing and timing preserve quick, agile movement.
- Each hand and foot has **four digits total**. Fingers and toes terminate in
  small, pointed claw-like protrusions; avoid large predatory or digging claws.
- Head armour reaches the forehead and ends in a widow's-peak contour. The
  cheeks remain soft and unarmoured so the feline front view and facial acting
  stay readable.
- Mane armour is moderately sized and should not protrude too far from the head
  and neck silhouette.
- Each shoulder has two to three larger, pad-like plates. Back armour connects
  directly to the mane and continues without a soft gap.
- The abdomen is unarmoured. Legs are half-wrapped: armoured on the outer face,
  unarmoured on the inner face.
- The tail is fully armoured.
- The dominant plate is a softened diamond / shield shape: rounded enough to
  feel organic, with a subtly pointed tip. Mane and shoulder plates remain
  closest to the Tela Aurea Lab logo diamonds, then transition toward natural
  pangolin teardrops over the back and tail.
- Plate size is smallest at the forehead, largest at the mane and shoulders,
  medium and regularly overlapped across the back, then gradually reduced toward
  the tail tip.
- Grayscale value order: soft skin near-white; armour a slightly cooler,
  one-step darker gray-white; plate seams and overlap shadows mid-gray; eyes,
  nose and only necessary outer contours the darkest gray.
- Tail length starts at **0.8× body length**.
- The hands are deliberately unarmoured. In the curled pose, their placement
  preserves the hollow centre of the Tela Aurea Lab logo.
- The base character is natural and unclothed, with no persistent accessory.
  Clothing, tools and props may be added per scene without becoming part of the
  model's default identity.
- Abdomen, cheeks, palms, inner legs and all other unarmoured regions use the
  same soft-surface material.
- No logo or permanent emblem appears on the body. The brand relationship is
  expressed through plate geometry and the curled logo pose only.
- It speaks. The voice can create contrast: sometimes cute and high, sometimes
  low and resonant. Exact vocal age, register and switching rule remain open.
- Its native appearance starts white and must read through grayscale, line,
  silhouette and value grouping. Colour is applied by scene or environment,
  not baked into the master asset.
- The character should eventually work across small reel-stage animation,
  stickers/illustration and 3D. For now, define the 2D and 3D appearance only.
- Curling up is the signature brand pose: the creature should resolve back
  toward the logo's concentric rings and tessellated diamonds.

### Open decisions

- Exact plate count and grouping. This must be simplified enough to read at
  about 200 px high and remain practical to rig in 3D.
- How the restrained mane is segmented: one radial collar, several articulated
  fans, or a size transition from the forehead crown to shoulder plates.
- Shoulder width, hand and foot size, and how far the torso leans during a fast
  upright run or walk. The 4.75-head baseline and short, powerful legs are
  settled, but can receive small silhouette-driven corrections.
- Hand anatomy within the four-digit limit: whether one digit is thumb-like or
  all four follow a paw/claw fan.
- How the unarmoured hands tuck or frame the face while retaining the logo's
  central negative space.
- Whether the rolling ball is a near-perfect circle or retains a readable tail
  spiral and mane rhythm.
- Voice contrast rule: emotional switch, deliberate performance, or a running
  gag that surprises other characters.
- Extreme-expression shape language and deformation limits.

## Design hypothesis to test first

Use an upright pangolin body plan and transformation logic, then place the lion
signal in three high-value areas:

1. a broad, readable chest/head silhouette;
2. a radial collar of flexible plates that behaves like a mane;
3. confident outward poses, balanced by curious and guileless facial acting.

This preserves the ability to curl without making the creature look like a lion
wearing armour. It also makes the mane a functional part of the transformation:
the plates fan out when expressive and nest into the concentric logo pose when
curled.

The head needs controlled view-dependent ambiguity. Keep the skull and muzzle
long enough to produce a pangolin profile, while using cat-like cheek width,
brow placement and nose scale to form a feline mask from the front. Do not solve
the front view by shortening the snout so much that the side view becomes a cat.

The tail's 0.8× body length should do real structural work: close the outside of
the curled silhouette and guide the eye around the logo spiral. The unarmoured
hands belong closer to the central opening, where their smoother value and shape
can separate the face from the surrounding plates.

At 4.75 heads tall, keep the leg mass in the thighs and calves rather than
lowering the pelvis so far that the character reads as a toddler. Small claws
should sharpen the silhouette only at close range; at reel size, hands and feet
must still read as clean four-digit shapes.

## Armour map

| Region | Coverage | Design purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crown / forehead | Armoured; ends in a widow's peak | Connects facial scales to the logo geometry while framing the eyes |
| Cheeks / muzzle | Unarmoured | Preserves the feline front read and expressive facial deformation |
| Mane / neck | Restrained flexible armour | Supplies the lion signal without overwhelming the head silhouette |
| Shoulders | Two to three larger pad-like plates per shoulder | Creates a regal shoulder line and a readable transition into the back |
| Back | Continuously armoured from the mane | Maintains the pangolin structure and supplies the outer curled shell |
| Abdomen | Unarmoured | Allows bending, compression and a clear soft-versus-armoured value break |
| Legs | Outer half armoured; inner half unarmoured | Protects the exterior silhouette while keeping fast leg motion flexible |
| Hands | Unarmoured | Supports expressive gestures and the hollow centre of the curled logo pose |
| Tail | Fully armoured | Completes the outer ring and protects the rolling surface |

The armour boundary should follow deformation needs, not look like clothing
seams. The forehead peak, shoulder pads and outer-leg panels are the three
graphic accents; the back and tail should feel like one continuous protective
system. During curl-up, the unarmoured abdomen and inner legs fold inward while
the back, outer legs and tail become the visible shell.

Avoid designing every natural scale. Establish a small number of large plate
groups, then imply finer scales with line and value. In 3D, each expressive mane
group needs a clear pivot and enough spacing to move without intersecting its
neighbours.

Do not imply fine scales everywhere. The target style is a simple, clean outline
with only the interior lines needed to explain armour regions and overlap. Avoid
surface texture, scratches, decorative hatching and repeated small-scale
linework. Forehead, mane, shoulders, back, outer legs and tail should each read
as a few grouped graphic masses before they read as individual plates.

The locomotion set now has three distinct movement modes:

1. upright idle — alert, lively and able to gesture with bare hands;
2. agile upright travel — a monster-like silhouette moving with quick steps,
   sharp acceleration and youthful forward drive;
3. hop–curl–roll — a quick, playful shortcut that reveals personality and
   transforms into the brand mark.

## Name decision

The final character name is **Telo（特羅）**.

The earlier exploration below is retained only as decision history, not as an
active shortlist.

The useful word parts are:

- `Tela`: web, fabric or canvas in the existing brand story;
- `Aurea`: golden / highest-standard in the existing brand story;
- `Leo`: Latin for lion;
- `pangolin`: from Malay `pengguling`, “one who rolls up”, which makes the
  transformation itself available as wordplay.

Three naming lanes:

### Short and character-like

- **Telo** — Tela + Leo; simple in Mandarin and English, but needs a collision
  check.
- **Lino** — lion + the tail of pangolin; soft and friendly, but quite generic.
- **Auri** — Aurea-derived; warm and shareable, though it hides the animal idea.

### Brand-linked hybrids

- **Telion** — Tela + lion; reads regal and clearly belongs to the brand.
- **Teloleo** — Tela + Leo with a playful repeated rhythm.
- **Telolin** — Tela + pangolin; softer and more creature-like.
- **Pangoleo** — the hybrid is immediately legible, but less proprietary.
- **Aureolin** — Aurea + pangolin; elegant and colour-related, but it is also
  the name of a yellow pigment, so that association must be intentional.

### Transformation-led

- **Guli / Guling** — taken from the “roll” root behind pangolin; strong concept,
  but cultural and pronunciation review is required before adoption.
- **Rollo / Rolléo** — roll + Leo; expressive but less directly Tela Aurea.

Do not select a name before checking spoken Traditional Chinese, English
pronunciation, domain/social collisions and trademark risk.

## Next discussion checkpoint

Stance, locomotion, the 12–15 age direction, face direction, tail baseline,
provisional 4.75-head proportions, the regional armour map and softened-diamond
plate language are settled enough for a new appearance exploration. The next
round must test simplified line density and the younger age read before exact
plate counts or production topology are decided.

The new visual exploration should be a clean grayscale silhouette-and-line
matrix rather than a rendered character sheet. It must include front,
three-quarter, side and curled views, plus one frame each from agile upright
travel and the hop–curl–roll sequence. A production-grade turnaround is a result
of that exploration, not an input to it.
